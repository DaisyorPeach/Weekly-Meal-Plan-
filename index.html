Monday
Ingredients
  • 500g pork mince
  • 400g hokkien noodles
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp oyster sauce
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp sesame oil (optional)
  • 2 cups frozen mixed veg
  • 1 tbsp oil
Method
  1. Cook noodles as per packet. Drain and set aside.
  2. Heat oil in a large pan or wok over high heat. Add pork mince and break up well. Cook 6–8 mins until browned.
  3. Add garlic powder and stir through.
  4. Add frozen veg straight from the bag. Cook 3 mins.
  5. Add noodles, soy sauce, oyster sauce and sesame oil. Toss everything together well over high heat for 2 mins.
  6. Serve immediately.
Really break up the mince while it cooks — you want fine crumbles, not clumps. High heat gives you a bit of char on the pork which is where the flavour comes from. Save a good serve for Tuesday's thermos.
Tuesday
Ingredients
  • 500g pork mince
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp tomato sauce
  • 1 tbsp cornflour mixed with 2 tbsp cold water
  • Frozen mash (as per packet)
Method
  1. Brown pork mince in a pan for 5 mins, breaking it up well. Drain any excess fat.
  2. Transfer to slow cooker. Add soy sauce, honey, garlic powder and tomato sauce. Stir well.
  3. Cook on LOW 4–6 hrs or HIGH 2–3 hrs.
  4. 30 mins before serving, stir in the cornflour slurry to thicken the sauce.
  5. Heat frozen mash as per packet. Spoon pork over mash to serve.
Browning the mince first gives much better depth of flavour than dumping it in raw. Don't skip it! The cornflour slurry at the end makes the sauce glossy and thick — restaurant-style.
Wednesday
Ingredients
  • 6 thick slices bread
  • 3 eggs
  • ½ cup milk
  • 1 tsp vanilla (optional)
  • 2 tbsp sugar + 1 tsp cinnamon, mixed
  • Butter for frying
Method
  1. Whisk eggs, milk and vanilla in a shallow bowl.
  2. Dip each bread slice, soaking 10 seconds each side.
  3. Fry in butter over medium heat, 2–3 mins each side until golden.
  4. Sprinkle cinnamon sugar over immediately while hot.
The sugar caramelises as it hits the hot bread — do it straight from the pan. A little extra butter on top as it melts is chef's kiss.
Ingredients
  • Half the lamb steaks (save half for Saturday)
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • Salt & pepper
  • Frozen mash (as per packet)
  • 2 cups frozen mixed veg
Method
  1. Season lamb with salt, pepper and garlic powder.
  2. Heat butter in a pan over high heat until foaming.
  3. Cook lamb 3–4 mins each side. Rest 3–4 mins off heat — cover with foil.
  4. Heat frozen mash and microwave veg as per packets.
  5. Slice lamb and serve over mash. Pour pan butter juices over everything.
Save the remaining lamb for Saturday's soy honey version. Two totally different flavour profiles from the same protein — one buttery and rich, one sweet and sticky.
Thursday — Easy Night ⚡
Ingredients
  • 500g pork mince
  • 400g tin diced tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp mixed Italian herbs
  • Salt & pepper
  • 400g penne pasta
  • Grated cheese to serve
Method
  1. Brown pork mince in a pan 5 mins. Drain fat.
  2. Transfer to slow cooker. Add tinned tomatoes, tomato paste, garlic powder and herbs. Stir.
  3. Cook LOW 4–6 hrs or HIGH 2–3 hrs.
  4. Cook penne as per packet when almost ready. Drain.
  5. Serve bolognese over penne with grated cheese.
Pork mince makes a wonderfully rich bolognese — often better than beef because of the higher fat content. Save a big serve for Friday's thermos lunch. It reheats beautifully.
Friday
Ingredients
  • Frozen burger patties (all of them)
  • Cheese slices
  • Burger buns, toasted in butter
  • Lettuce, sauce & toppings of choice
Method
  1. Heat a pan over high heat. Cook patties from frozen — about 5–6 mins each side until cooked through.
  2. Add cheese slice right after flipping so it melts while the second side cooks.
  3. Toast buns in a separate pan with butter.
  4. Assemble and serve immediately.
Cooking from frozen is absolutely fine — just make sure the inside is cooked through. Don't press the patties down while cooking or you squeeze all the juices out.
Saturday
Ingredients
  • 1 packet pancake mix (or 1½ cups SR flour, 2 eggs, 1 cup milk, 1 tbsp sugar)
  • Butter for frying
  • Butter & maple syrup to serve
Method
  1. Make batter as per packet, or whisk SR flour, eggs, milk and sugar until smooth with no lumps.
  2. Rest batter 5 mins — this makes fluffier pancakes.
  3. Heat pan over medium heat with a small knob of butter.
  4. Pour in about ¼ cup of batter per pancake. Cook until bubbles form and edges look set — about 2 mins. Flip and cook 1 min more.
  5. Keep warm in oven at lowest temp while cooking the rest.
  6. Stack high and serve with butter and maple syrup.
Resting the batter is the secret to thick fluffy pancakes. Don't overmix — a few lumps are fine. Medium heat, not high, so they cook through without burning.
Ingredients
  • Remaining lamb steaks
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Frozen mash (as per packet)
  • 2 cups frozen mixed veg
Method
  1. Mix soy, honey and garlic powder. Coat lamb steaks and marinate 10+ mins.
  2. Heat oil in a pan over medium-high. Cook lamb 3–4 mins each side, spooning marinade over as it cooks.
  3. Rest 3 mins. Heat mash and microwave veg as per packets.
  4. Slice lamb, serve over mash with veg. Pour pan juices over.
Watch the heat once the honey hits the pan — it caramelises fast and can burn. Medium-high is enough. The dark caramelised bits on the pan make the best sauce — scrape them up with a little splash of water.
Sunday
Ingredients
  • 2 sheets frozen puff pastry, thawed
  • 3 tbsp softened butter
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 1½ tsp cinnamon
  • Optional icing: ½ cup icing sugar + 1 tbsp milk
Method
  1. Preheat oven to 160°C. Mix sugar and cinnamon together in a small bowl.
  2. Lay pastry sheets on a floured bench. Kids spread softened butter all over with a spoon or butter knife.
  3. Kids sprinkle the cinnamon sugar all over the butter — as much as they like!
  4. Roll up tightly from one long edge into a log. Press the seam to seal.
  5. Cut into 6–8 rounds per sheet. Place flat on a lined baking tray.
  6. Bake at 160°C for 22–26 mins until puffed and golden.
  7. Cool slightly. Drizzle icing over if using.
At 160°C they take a little longer than usual but puff pastry is very forgiving. The kids can do almost all of this — spreading, sprinkling, rolling and pressing. Let them go wild with the cinnamon sugar!
Ingredients
  • 400g penne
  • 2 × 185g tins tuna, well drained
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • ½ cup grated cheese
  • Salt & pepper
Method
  1. Cook penne as per packet. Drain, reserving a splash of pasta water.
  2. Return to pot over low heat. Add butter and toss until melted.
  3. Stir through tuna gently, keeping some chunks.
  4. Add cheese and toss. Splash pasta water if it looks dry. Season and serve.
Ingredients
  • Basa fillets (full pack)
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • Salt & pepper
  • Frozen mash (as per packet)
  • 2 cups frozen mixed veg
Method
  1. Pat basa dry. Season with salt, pepper and garlic powder.
  2. Heat butter in a pan over medium-high until foaming.
  3. Cook basa 3–4 mins each side until golden. Squeeze lemon over at the end.
  4. Heat mash and microwave veg as per packets.
  5. Serve fish over mash, spoon pan butter over everything.
Don't move the fish while it cooks — let it get a golden crust. It'll release from the pan naturally when it's ready to flip. Basa is a very forgiving fish that kids tend to love.

Splitting the pork mince across the week
You have 1.5kg pork mince for 3 meals — roughly 500g each. Monday stir fry (500g), Tuesday slow cooker honey garlic (500g), Thursday slow cooker bolognese (500g). Weigh them out roughly on Monday when you get home from shopping so you're not guessing later.
Splitting the lamb steaks
Use half the lamb Wednesday (garlic butter, simple and fast) and save the rest for Saturday (soy honey marinade, stickier and richer). Two completely different dinners from the one pack. If you're not sure how many steaks you have, count them and split evenly.
Slow cooker tip — both days
Both Tuesday and Thursday use the slow cooker. For both, brown the pork mince in a pan first — this one step makes an enormous difference to the depth of flavour. Takes 5 extra mins but the result is completely different to dumping it in raw. Set it in the morning and forget about it.
Sunday cinnamon scrolls with the kids
The kids can do basically everything — spread the butter, dump the cinnamon sugar on (let them go wild), roll it up with your help, and press the seam. Cut the rolls yourself as the knife is the only dangerous part. At 160°C they take about 22–26 mins — check at 20 mins. They should be golden and puffed.
Thursday lunch — rice for the kids!
This is the week you try the kids on rice again. Keep the serve small and pair it with the lamb which has good flavour — plain rice on its own is harder to get kids interested in. A tiny drizzle of soy sauce helps a lot. Don't make a big deal of it either way.
Thermos lunches this week
Pre-heat the thermos every morning — boiling water in for 5 mins, pour out, fill with piping hot food. Tuesday pork noodles, Wednesday honey garlic pork on noodles, Thursday lamb & rice, Friday bolognese penne — all hold heat beautifully. Prep the night before takes under 10 mins each time.
